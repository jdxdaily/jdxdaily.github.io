US equities rally on earnings beat and easing inflation data

A wider read of yesterday's session — sector rotation, pre-market action, watchlist scan, and what to watch into the US open at 21:30 HKT.

10-minute read · Topics: Macro · Geopolitics · Earnings · Sectors · Watchlist Download PDF

TL;DR

The major indices closed higher on Friday, led by a +0.83% jump in the Dow Jones and a +0.59% rise in the S&P 500. The rally was anchored by strong earnings from AMZN and a modest dip in the VIX to 14.84, indicating reduced near‑term volatility.

Behind the headline gains, the market is pricing in a slower pace of rate hikes after the latest 10‑year Treasury yield slipped to 5.24. However, the upside is limited by a still‑elevated inflation backdrop and a modest pullback in energy prices.

Spotlight: Amazon’s earnings surprise

AMZN delivered a +3.29% post‑market jump after reporting Q3 revenue of 149.2 bn, 12% above consensus. Cloud services grew 18% YoY, driven by AI‑enhanced offerings. The company also announced a new logistics hub in Southeast Asia, adding a strategic foothold for regional growth.

The FOMC left rates unchanged at 5.25‑5.50% and signaled a “patient” stance, noting inflation is trending down but remains above target. The 10‑year yield settled at 5.24, flattening the curve between 2‑year and 5‑year maturities.

What could break the tape

BEARISH TRIGGERS
  • Unexpected rise in CPI above 0.3% MoM could reignite rate‑hike expectations.
  • Energy price shock from geopolitical escalation, pushing WTI above 100.
  • Weakness in tech earnings, especially a miss from NVDA or MSFT.
BULLISH TRIGGERS
  • Continued earnings beat across the “Magnificent Seven”, reinforcing growth narrative.
  • Further decline in the VIX below 12, indicating risk‑on sentiment.
  • Positive surprise in core CPI, prompting Fed to maintain dovish stance.

Positioning notes

  • Weight toward AI‑enabled tech and cloud names as earnings momentum persists.
  • Maintain a modest defensive tilt via utilities (XLU) and health‑care (XLV) given volatility backdrop.
  • Monitor the 10‑year yield; a break above 5.30 could pressure growth stocks.
