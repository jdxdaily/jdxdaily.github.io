US equities slip as yields rise and energy gains offset tech weakness

A wider read of yesterday's session — sector rotation, pre-market action, watchlist scan, and what to watch into the US open at 21:30 HKT.

10-minute read · Topics: Macro · Geopolitics · Earnings · Sectors · Watchlist Download PDF

TL;DR

The S&P 500 fell -0.5% to 7,623.68 while the Dow slid -1.2%. Higher 10‑year yields at 4.99% and a rally in oil (WTI +3.8%) pressured growth‑sensitive sectors, yet the Nasdaq held near flat as AI‑related stocks held ground.

Behind the headline, the market is digesting a mixed macro backdrop: a firmer yield curve, a modest rise in the VIX to 19.45, and a softening of consumer‑discretionary ETFs. The biggest risk remains a potential acceleration in Fed tightening if inflation data stays above expectations.

Macro & Fed

The Fed’s September meeting left rates unchanged at 5.25‑5.50% but signaled a “higher for longer” stance. Minutes highlighted persistent core‑inflation pressures, keeping the door open for a possible 25‑bp hike in November.

Yield curve remains steep: 2‑year at 5.12%, 10‑year at 4.99%, 30‑year at 5.45%.

What could break the tape

BEARISH TRIGGERS
  • U.S. CPI surprise above 0.3% – could prompt another rate hike.
  • Further escalation in the Red Sea – risk to global shipping and energy supply.
  • Unexpected downgrade of major tech earnings (e.g., NVDA or MSFT).
BULLISH TRIGGERS
  • WTI breaches 110 – fuels broader market rally.
  • Positive surprise in U.S. retail sales – supports consumer‑driven sectors.
  • Resolution of U.S.–China trade talks – lifts semiconductor stocks.

Positioning notes

  • Shift weight toward energy and inflation‑protected assets as yields stay elevated.
  • Maintain a defensive bias in consumer staples and utilities to hedge rate risk.
  • Selective exposure to AI‑driven tech remains attractive despite short‑term volatility.
