US equities tumble as yields rise and tech sells off

A wider read of yesterday's session — sector rotation, pre-market action, watchlist scan, and what to watch into the US open at 21:30 HKT.

10-minute read · Topics: Macro · Geopolitics · Earnings · Sectors · Watchlist Download PDF

TL;DR

The S&P 500 slipped -1.09% to 7,659.44, driven by a broad tech sell‑off and a jump in the 10‑year Treasury yield to 5.10%. The VIX surged +21.45%, signaling heightened volatility ahead of the Fed’s next policy decision.

While defensive sectors such as financials and energy posted modest gains, the technology sector remains the engine of weakness, with the Nasdaq 100 down -2.50%. Investors should watch the Fed’s upcoming commentary and the upcoming earnings window for catalysts that could reverse the current risk‑off bias.

Spotlight: 10‑year Treasury yield spikes

The 10‑year Treasury yield rose +0.18% to 5.10%, its highest level since early 2024. The move reflects the Fed’s “higher‑for‑longer” narrative after the latest FOMC minutes and a stronger demand for cash assets.

Higher yields pressure rate‑sensitive equities, especially technology and growth‑oriented REITs. Fixed‑income investors may find opportunities in short‑duration bonds as the curve flattens.

Macro & Fed

FOMC minutes released Tuesday showed a consensus for two more 25‑bp hikes before a potential pause. The Fed’s “higher‑for‑longer” stance is reinforcing the yield curve’s flattening, with the 2‑year at 5.30% and the 10‑year at 5.10%.

What could break the tape

BEARISH TRIGGERS
  • Further 10‑year yield rise above 5.20% triggering margin calls in tech.
  • Unexpected CPI acceleration > 0.4% MoM.
  • Escalation of Middle‑East conflict disrupting oil supply.
BULLISH TRIGGERS
  • Fed signals a pause or rate cut in the next meeting.
  • Tech earnings beat expectations, especially AI revenue guidance.
  • Strong retail sales data showing consumer resilience.

Positioning notes

  • Shift weight toward short‑duration fixed income and defensive equities as yields climb.
  • Maintain selective exposure to AI‑related names with strong balance sheets; avoid inventory‑heavy chipmakers.
  • Monitor currency dynamics – a weaker DXY supports commodity exporters and emerging‑market equities.
