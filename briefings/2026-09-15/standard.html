US equities slip as tech drags, volatility spikes

A wider read of yesterday's session — sector rotation, pre-market action, watchlist scan, and what to watch into the US open at 21:30 HKT.

10-minute read · Topics: Macro · Geopolitics · Earnings · Sectors · Watchlist Download PDF

TL;DR

All major US indices closed lower on September 14, with the NVDA sell‑off dragging the Nasdaq 100 to a -0.82% decline. The VIX surged +7.95% to 17.10, signalling heightened risk appetite after the Fed’s dovish tone.

Behind the headline, the market is digesting three layers: (1) the Fed’s “no‑rush” stance after the latest rate cut, (2) a broad rotation into consumer‑discretionary and communication services ETFs, and (3) rising commodity prices that could pressure inflation‑sensitive sectors. Investors should watch whether the tech correction deepens or stabilises before the next earnings wave.

Spotlight: Fed’s “no‑rush” stance after latest rate cut

The Federal Open Market Committee left the policy rate unchanged at 5.25‑5.50% and signalled a pause, citing inflation’s gradual decline. The market reacted with a modest yield dip and a spike in the VIX, reflecting uncertainty over the timing of the next move.

What could break the tape

BEARISH TRIGGERS
  • Further Fed tightening if core CPI stalls above 3 %.
  • Escalation of Middle‑East conflict pushing oil above $110, spurring inflation.
  • Tech earnings miss, especially NVDA or AMD, reigniting sector sell‑off.
BULLISH TRIGGERS
  • Strong Q3 earnings from AI‑driven names (GOOGL, MSFT).
  • Positive CPI surprise (core < 2.8 %) prompting a rate‑cut expectation.
  • Continued commodity price moderation easing inflation concerns.

Positioning notes

  • Maintain a core long exposure to communication services (XLC) as ad spend recovers.
  • Scale back pure‑play semiconductor exposure; rotate into diversified tech (MSFT, AAPL).
  • Allocate a modest hedge to volatility via VIX futures or inverse ETFs given the recent spike.
